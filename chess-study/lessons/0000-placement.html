Think of the first visit to a physiotherapist

Before they prescribe a single exercise they ask you to bend forward, reach up, and stand on one leg. Three small movements, no judgement, and they are not checking whether you do them perfectly: each one tells them something different about what to work on first. A plan written before that visit would be a plan for somebody else.

In chess the same first visit is called a placement: three positions, one move each, and the reasons you give are worth more than the moves. There is nothing to study before this page. Just answer honestly, without an engine and without hints.

What did their last move leave behind?

A club game out of your own opening, 1.e4. Both sides have developed, Black put a bishop on g4 to pin your knight, you nudged it with 7.h3, and Black replied by castling.

Say your move and, in one or two sentences, why.

Explain in your own words

      The last pawn

      Everything else has been traded. White has one pawn left and a king in front of it; Black's king stands in the way. White has six legal moves and exactly one of them wins. The other five draw.

        That is the whole placement. There is no score on this page and nothing to pass: the coach reads your three answers, the moves and above all the reasons, and places you in a band. Then comes the interview (your rating and where it comes from, how your games are usually lost, what you have already studied), and the real lessons start from where you actually are, not from page one of a book.

        Where the positions come from

        Position 1 was composed for this placement from a typical club game in the Italian Game (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.d3 Bc5 5.O-O d6 6.c3 Bg4 7.h3 O-O). Position 2 is Morphy – Duke of Brunswick and Count Isouard, Paris 1858 (the Opera Game), after 15...Nxd7. Position 3 is the first king-and-pawn ending of every endgame primer (Silman's Complete Endgame Course, Part One; Lichess Practice, "Key squares" and "Opposition"). Every position and every claim on this page was checked with Stockfish through scripts/engine_check.py before it was written down.
