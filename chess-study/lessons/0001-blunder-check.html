Lesson 1 · I stop hanging pieces

Look both ways: the check before every move

What did their last move attack, what can I take, what checks are there. Three questions, every move, before anything else.

Think of crossing a road

Nobody decides to look before stepping off the kerb. You look left, you look right, you look left again, and you do it on the street you cross every day, at the quiet hour, when you are sure nothing is coming. It takes two seconds. It is not cleverness, it is a habit that runs before the thought of where you are going. The one crossing where you skip it, because you were thinking about something more interesting, is the only one that ever matters.

In chess the thing you are looking for is a hanging piece: a piece left where it can be taken for nothing. The three looks before every move are the blunder check. Under 1000, almost every game is decided by it, in one direction or the other, and nothing else in this study is worth learning until it runs by itself.

1

What can I take? The hanging piece

A piece is hanging when it is attacked and nobody defends it: whoever takes it gets it for free. The values make "free" exact: pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. A knight taken for a pawn is a loss of two, and at this level a loss of two is usually the game.

Here is how it happens. White has played the first four moves of the Italian Game and Black, seeing a pawn on e4, took it: 4...Nxe4. Black asked "what can I take?" and stopped there. The question that was missing is the one from White's side: e4 was defended by the pawn on d3, so the knight that took it now stands where a pawn can take it back, and nothing of Black's defends e4.

5.dxe4 collects a knight for a pawn. Stockfish says +3.9 for White after it, and every other move throws most of that away: 5.O-O lets the knight walk home and loses 4.5 pawns of advantage; 5.Nc3 even loses a pawn after 5...Nxc3. So the first look, before every move of yours: every capture you have, and is it free? Attacked and undefended is free. Attacked by a pawn is nearly always free whatever defends it, because a pawn is the cheapest thing on the board.

Now the same look, with nothing marked. Same opening, one move later than a game you could play tomorrow: Black has just put a knight on g4, aiming at f2 together with the bishop on c5.

Explain in your own words

White to move. What do you play, and why? Say which piece is hanging, what attacks it, and what defends it.

    2

    What did their last move attack?

    The second look is about them. Every move changes what a piece attacks, so the first thing to look at is the piece that just moved: which squares does it reach now, and is one of yours standing on one of them?

    Here it is after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6. The a-pawn has moved one square, and that square attacks b5. The bishop that was pinning the knight a move ago is now the thing under attack, and whatever plan you had waits. Two answers keep the bishop: 4.Ba4 (the engine's choice, +0.4) and 4.Bxc6 (trade it for the knight, +0.1). Any move that ignores the pawn hands the bishop over: 4.O-O?? axb5 and White is a piece down for nothing, a swing of four and a half pawns. 4.Nc3?? and 4.d4?? lose it the same way.

    The third look is the checks: yours and theirs. A check has to be answered before anything else, so a move that gives check and takes something, or that lands a queen next to your king, is the most dangerous move on the board, and the one most often missed. Here you are Black, the board is turned your way, and White has just played 3.Bc4 after bringing the queen out early. Run the second and third looks: what did that bishop just attack, and what check does White have next move?

    Pick one

    Black to move. What did White's last move attack, what check does White have next move, and what do you play?

    3

    Before you let go of the piece: the same three looks, from their chair

    The check is not finished when you have found a move. Play it in your head, then change chairs and ask the three questions as the opponent: what did that move just attack, what can they take now, what checks do they have. If any answer is "a piece for free" or "a check that wins something", the move goes back and you look for another.

    The usual failure here is a capture or a check that feels strong. After 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6, 4.Bxf7+ gives check and takes a pawn, and both feel like progress. Change chairs. After 4...Kxf7, what can White take? Nothing. What checks does White have? 5.Ng5+, and the king just steps away. White has given a bishop (3) for a pawn (1). Stockfish: -3.3 for White after 4.Bxf7+ Kxf7, a loss of 3.7 pawns against the quiet 4.d3. 4.Nxe5?? is the same mistake with the knight: 4...Nxe5, because the e5 pawn was defended all along. The right moves are the boring ones: 4.d3, 4.Nc3 and 4.O-O all keep every piece safe and stay within 0.7 of the engine's best. (Its own first choices are 4.d3 and the sharp 4.Ng5; that does not matter here. What matters is that nothing you own can be taken for free after your move.)

    Now a position from the same opening a few moves on, with nothing marked. Both sides have castled. White has two tempting moves and several quiet ones; run the three looks on whatever you want to play, from Black's chair, before you answer.

    Explain in your own words

    White to move. What do you play, and why? Name at least one tempting move you rejected, and what the look from Black's chair told you about it.

        Before every move

        A piece is hanging when...

        ...it is attacked and nothing defends it, or a pawn attacks it. Whoever takes it gets it for free, or for a pawn.

        Piece values

        Pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. A knight for a pawn is minus two, and under 1000 minus two is usually the game.

        Question 1, before every move

        What did their last move attack? Look at the piece that just moved and every square it now reaches.

        Question 2, before every move

        What can I take, and is it free? Every capture on the board, and what defends the square.

        Question 3, and then the switch

        What checks are there, mine and theirs? Then play your move in your head and ask all three again from their chair: what did I just attack, what can they take, what checks do they have.
