# Ideation record: how the team got to Hold On

## The partners and their perspectives

### Chess coach: master-strength player, twenty years coaching club players from their first games to 2000+

Under 1000 a rating moves for one reason only: the player stops leaving pieces where they can be taken, and that is a habit built in the half-second before the piece lands, not an understanding gained from an evaluation afterwards. What a live coach does that no app has done is sit beside the student, stop their hand, ask one question ("what defends that square?"), let them play the bad move when they insist and feel the recapture, come back to the same mistake next week in a fresh position, and say nothing at all about the ninety percent of moves that do not matter at their level.

- **Hold on** — A game against a beginner-strength engine where the coach stops your hand before a hanging move lands, asks one question, and lets you take it back or play it anyway and feel the punishment.
- **Their chair** — Two-sided puzzles from real beginner games: first sit in the opponent's chair and punish the move, then come back to your own chair, one move earlier, and find the move that would never have allowed it.
- **Your three** — After every game the coach pulls the three moments that decided it, verifies each with the engine, and asks you about them next time, in the same position and then in a new one, until that mistake stops happening.

### Product designer: intuitiveness, craft and emotional design for the LLM-native chess coach, designing for Sambudh (under 1000, studied nothing, opens 1.e4, phone in hand) first.

Product design cares that the first screen is a board with one line of text and nothing to learn, that the single primary action at every moment is "make a move" or "tap the board", and that the visual identity reads as chess rather than as an AI app: a tournament board (buff and green squares), the coach speaking in a book serif, no eval bar, no cards, no scores, both themes at 400 px with the board as the one hero. Above everything it cares how a blunder feels: here the piece is held before it lands and the player chooses, so a mistake becomes a question you answered instead of a red mark you received after the fact.

- **Hold On** — You play a game; when your move is about to lose a piece the board holds it in the air and asks you one question about the position, which you answer by tapping a square. Then you decide.
- **Chairs** — A five-minute daily drill where you make a move, the board turns around, and you try to punish your own move from the other side with your own hands.
- **Ghost Moves** — A playground where any move you pick up is shown as a ghost before it lands: what it attacks, what it leaves hanging, what they would play back, drawn on the board, with 'Why?' one tap away.

### LLM engineer

Claude earns its place only where language and memory matter: turning an engine threat into one sentence a beginner feels, asking the question a coach would ask, grading a reason rather than a move, and remembering what this player keeps doing; every chess fact it utters must be computed by chess.js and the in-page Stockfish, placed in the prompt as a fact sheet before the call, and checked again on the way out (every square and SAN token in the reply validated against the fact sheet, else the page's own template line shows). Quick tier with zero tool rounds for anything that happens during a move, default tier once per game while the player is already looking at a result screen, calls only from the player's own actions, and the page must play, talk and drill fully from engine-derived templates when `claude.use("sample")` is null or any call rejects.

- **Open Hand** — Play against an opponent of your own level who tells you, in one sentence, what it is about to do to you, so you learn to see it before it says it.
- **The One Thing** — After each game your biggest mistake becomes one question in a coach's words, graded on your reasoning, and the page remembers which mistake you keep making.
- **Point at It** — Tap any piece and ask the only three questions a beginner has about it; the answer is one sentence drawn on the board, never wrong, and sometimes a question back.

### The beginner: Sambudh's stand-in. Rated under 1000 once, studied nothing, opens 1.e4, lost most games to pieces taken for free without knowing why, and has closed every chess app that showed him an evaluation bar.

I do not want a number, a label or a line of moves; I want one plain sentence and a lit-up square at the exact moment it matters, which is the second before I let go of a piece and the second after I lose one. I will come back tomorrow if the thing being counted is one I understand and can feel going down (pieces I gave away today, not an ELO estimate), and I will send it to a friend if there is a single moment I can hand them with "can you see it?"

- **Hold On** — A game against a bot where the board catches your hand before you give a piece away, and lets you argue back.
- **Rewind** — After a loss, not a report card: the one moment it went wrong, a question instead of an answer, and the chance to play the game again from there.
- **Can You See It** — One position, one question, one tap, every day, from your own games; and a link that puts a friend in the same seat.

### Game designer

A game designer cares that the thing the player does every twenty seconds is itself the lesson, that the opponent is tuned so a beginner can win while losing exactly the way they need to lose, and that getting better is felt in the hands (a ring that shrinks, a chair that is no longer offered) before it is ever shown as a number. The coach is a voice inside the game that arrives at the moment it is asked for, points at the board, says one true thing in the player's words, and otherwise stays out of the way.

- **Both Ways** — Play an opponent that tells you what it is about to do, and learn to look before it has to.
- **Change Chairs** — When you hang a piece, the opponent stops the game and hands you its chair: take it yourself, feel it, then rewind.
- **Three Looks, Daily** — One position a day from a game like yours: tap what their last move attacks, what you can take, what checks there are; share your three squares.

### Market skeptic and competitive analyst

Stockfish facts turned into LLM prose with a follow-up chat is now a commodity: Chessiro, ChessGPT, LearnChess, Chesska, the Lichess AI Coach extension and Chess.com Game Review v2 all ship it, all post-game or hint-shaped, and a 2026 benchmark still finds about a fifth of commentary sub-claims wrong without tools, so engine grounding is the entry fee, not a feature, and "chat next to a board" is dead on arrival. What no product does is listen: the player says the check before the move, the engine supplies the facts in milliseconds, and the LLM's one necessary job is to find the false belief in the player's own sentence and ask the question that exposes it without naming the move; that reversal is the interaction a Lichess forum thread would call new, and the other two concepts here (the rationed look from the opponent's chair, the habit that whispers before the moment) are its best companions, not substitutes.

- **Say It Before You Move** — You play a bot that hangs pieces like an 800, and before each move you say your check in one line; the coach stays silent when your reasoning is complete and asks one question, never a move, when it is not.
- **Their Chair** — Before you commit a move, tap once, the board turns round, and your opponent tells you in one sentence what it would do to that move; you get three such looks a game, and a clean look is refunded.
- **What You Keep Doing** — The coach remembers your last games as up to three named habits, in your own words, and in the next game whispers the habit before the moment it usually happens, not after.

### Frontend architect

The board is the whole interface: every answer, from the engine or from Claude, has to land on the squares (a held piece, a pulsing square, a ghost line, a square turning green) within a frame on a 400 px phone with one primary action, which means pieces as an absolutely positioned inline-SVG layer animated by transform, pointer events handling tap and drag in one code path, and the Stockfish Worker warmed at load so the player never waits on it. The second thing I care about is seams: plain ES modules with one engine.js command queue, one coach.js where `sample` resolves to `window.__stubSample` before `claude.use('sample')`, and one store.js that is `db`+`user` inside the viewer and localStorage everywhere else, so the page plays fully with no Claude at all and the existing headless checker (scripts/app_check.js) can drive, assert and screenshot every flow.

- **Hold On** — Play a game against a soft Stockfish; when a move would hang something, the piece is held on the board instead of landing, and the coach asks one question you answer by tapping a square.
- **Show Me** — Tap a piece and ask 'what if this takes?'; Claude tries your idea with Stockfish and answers by playing the line out on your board as ghost pieces, then puts everything back.
- **Say It First** — On the moves that matter, you say the three looks in one line before you move; Claude reads what you meant, the board checks it against the truth, and the move unlocks when your looking was honest.

## The critics

### feasibility — verdict: revise

Strongest part: The silent gate and the engine pipeline are real, not aspirational: running the actual vendored Stockfish 18 lite WASM worker in headless Chromium at the spec's exact depths, all thirteen (position, move) pairs the acceptance names produce the verdict the spec predicts (Nxe5 423, Bxf7+ 391, h3 42 in the 6...d6 position; tutorial Bxf7+ 278, Nxe5 271, d3 8; Ruy O-O 411 with reply axb5, Ba4 1; Nc3 542 with pre-search best dxe4 of a pawn-attacked knight, dxe4 -6; Nbd2 289 and O-O 288 with the pre-search best being the free capture; g4 answered by Qh4 mate 1; Qh3 after a pre-search mate 1), every 'clean' move under 50 cp, every best reply a capture exactly where the category needs one, and the whole batch in 1.9 s with a 368 ms warm-up. The pure-function gate over chess.js attackers() (which does report defenders, verified in the vendored source) plus a fully template-driven no-Claude path means the product is playable, testable and honest before a single sample call exists, which is the right order for a one-session build.

Must change:
- Rewrite validate.js as an ordered pipeline and fix the regex: (1) extract every [a-h][1-8] token, require it to be in the allowed set (and, for K1's question, not in the answer set), then mask those tokens; (2) SAN check on the masked text with a regex that requires a piece letter, an 'x', a '=' or O-O (the spec's regex matches bare squares and rejects its own example outputs; verified by running it); (3) number check on the masked text; (4) banned words; (5) word caps. Add a unit test that the spec's two example_output JSONs pass.
- Define the gate categories so the acceptance is reachable: hanging_after_move only when the piece the best reply captures is the piece he just moved (or became attackable because of the move); ignored_attack when it is a different piece that chess.js already saw attacked before the move; add the 'moved the defender' case to ignored_attack with its own template ('Hold on. Something of yours just lost its guard. Tap it.'). Under the spec's stated priority, the Ruy O-O test (cpLoss 411, reply axb5) fires hanging_after_move, never ignored_attack.
- Make the tutorial hold engine-independent: detect Bxf7+ / Nxe5 by SAN match on the known FEN with canned answer sets, no verdict search; and state the cold-engine policy for real moves (while the Worker is not ready, the player's move commits silently with no verdict and the bot uses the book or 'Warming up the other side...'). The spec contradicts itself ('the gate runs as normal' vs 'This hold is canned') and the phone's 2-4 s warm-up plus a 7.3 MB download lands exactly on the 3-8 s first hold.
- Reconcile the test plan with /home/user/hello-world/scripts/app_check.js: export one async (page, ctx) function that runs the scenarios in sequence (it runs four times, fresh page each time); add to the harness STUB_SCRIPT an outcome field per recorded call (resolved / cancelled / error code) and an error-injection hook (window.__stubErrors consumed by the stub, not by coach.js); drop 'the stub reports the K1 call as cancelled' unless that is done; replace the bot-as-White dxe4 test with a Black-to-move position (after Nxe5 in the 6...d6 position: dxe5 10/10), since playing Black is iteration two; add a window.__app.fast flag that zeroes animation durations so 45 assertions x 4 runs stay under ~3 minutes.
- Restate the 'Looked.' chip rule in FEN-derivable terms (a non-pawn piece of his attacked-and-undefended or by a cheaper piece before the move, nothing hanging after) because load(fen) has no 'their last move'; keep the 6...d6 h3 negative test to prove it stays quiet.
- Fix the copy guard so it does not fail on validate.js's own ban list or on stub fixtures: exclude app/src/validate.js (or assemble the words from a regex/char codes) and scripts/scenarios/*.
- Cut the one-session scope explicitly and in this order of what ships: tap-tap only (drag is the enhancement, keep its code path out of v1), auto-queen promotion (strip later), opening book of 6-8 lines, close card with text captions instead of mini static boards, the gift rule behind a GIFT_RATE constant that defaults to 0 until a 20-game headless smoke exists (there is no time in-session for 'a few hundred self-play games'), and a piece set decision made before coding: the repo has nothing to reuse (/home/user/hello-world/chess-study/components/local/board.js renders Unicode GLYPHs), so commit to twelve chunky geometric silhouettes (circle/trapezoid/cross builds, no 'hand-drawn' ambition) with a fixed two-hour cap.

### Beginner value — verdict: revise

Strongest part: The held move itself, and the fact that it is exactly the chess-master's curriculum turned into a thumb gesture. The four categories map one-to-one onto the under-1000 band's three looks plus the 'change chairs' check from lesson 0001 p3 (ignored_attack = what did their last move attack; free_piece_ignored = what can I take; allowed_mate and missed_mate = what checks are there; hanging_after_move = play it in your head and look from their chair), nothing outside that band ever holds, and the whole beat (lift, amber square, dimmed board, 'Hold on. ... Tap it.', graded by chess.js, 'Take it back' with no mark, 'Show me' with the ghost and the '-2') works with zero LLM calls and no reading beyond one sentence. The first screen is a board and one line, which a beginner parses in under five seconds, and 'Show me' is the best single piece of feedback in the spec: watching the future happen on your own board and then undo itself is understood by someone who has never heard the word centipawn. If the opponent is fixed so he can win and gets pieces to take, this core is the right thing to build.

Must change:
- The opponent must be calibrated by measurement, not by adjectives, and the gift mechanism must be rebuilt so it actually fires. Replace 'plays like an 800' with an acceptance test: over 100 headless self-play games against Stockfish with UCI_LimitStrength true and UCI_Elo 1320 at depth 8, the Hold On bot scores at most 40 percent and leaves at least two depth-10-verified free minor pieces per game on average (the current recipe scores 100 percent and leaves zero). Concretely: (1) generate gift candidates by enumerating the bot's legal moves with chess.js and keeping those after which a bot minor piece is attacked by the player and undefended or attacked by a pawn, then verify one with a depth-10 search, instead of looking inside the six best lines where such moves never are; (2) allow gifts across the whole game, not only bot moves 5-25, and allow a gift to be a recapture or a careless capture, which is how an 800 actually loses pieces; (3) widen the sampler (80 cp to about 250 cp, or Skill Level 0-3 for non-forced moves) and re-measure; (4) keep 'always takes yours' exactly as written, it is the one part that works. Keep the gift rate and window as two constants and ship the self-play script with the repo.
- Give him a way out of a lost game. The spec has no control between the first move and the close card (information_architecture lists none; persistence says 'a reload starts a new game'), the bot resigns only when it is losing, and the self-play shows he will be the one losing. Add a quiet text link 'Give up' (ink, never red, next to the theme dot or at the end of the coach line) with a one-tap confirm inside the serif line ('Give up this one? [Yes] [Keep playing]'), reaching the same close card with result 'You gave up' and all the holds counted; and a mirror of the bot's mercy: when he has been nine or more points down for three moves, the line offers 'Start again?' once. Without this his 10-15 minutes end in a position he cannot leave, and the close card, the count and the remembered line, the product's entire reason to come back, never appear.
- The gate must never go silent on the two things that end his games. ### novelty — verdict: revise

Strongest part: The held move whose answer is a tap: the engine decides the moment, the piece does not land, the question withholds the answer square, chess.js grades the tap instantly, and Claude only phrases. I searched for a product that asks 'what takes it? Tap it.' on a suspended move in a live game and found none; Dr. Wolf asks yes/no and names the problem, Play Coach lights the pieces for you, Fritz offers the tap only as a training drill, and the square-colouring products show rather than ask. Combined with 'zero sample calls in a clean game' and a validator that makes a wrong square physically unable to reach the board, that is a genuinely new interaction and not a chat box; the spec's problem is that it wraps this true claim in several false ones.

Must change:
- Rewrite the novelty claim in thesis, tagline and the_new_interaction to what the evidence supports and nothing more: 'the only chess software that suspends your move, asks a question you answer by tapping the board, never names the answer or the move, and otherwise says nothing.' Add the missing incumbents to the thesis paragraph with one clause each on what they do and the one thing they do not: Dr. Wolf (interrupts, asks 'Are you certain?', take-back, flip sides; names the problem, yes/no answer), Shredder and Fritz coach (warn and offer help; show the move), Fritz Assisted Analysis / ChessUp / Chess.com Highlighter (colour squares before the drop; show, do not ask), Play Coach (withholds the move, lights pieces, retries; talks every move, nothing is held), Chessiro (typed question grounded by Stockfish with arrows; after the game). Delete the words 'first' and 'nothing in the world'.
- Make K2 ('But...') a board question or cut it from the MVP. The reply contract becomes {say, ask: {kind, squares}} where kind is one of a small enumerated set whose answer set facts.js has already computed from chess.js (e.g. 'square_the_held_piece_can_reach_that_attacks_target_and_no_pawn_reaches', 'piece_that_takes_on_square', 'what_takes_back'); the prompt lists the kinds available for this hold with their answer sets; validate.js rejects a reply whose kind is not offered or whose answer set is empty; the tap after a K2 reply is graded by chess.js exactly like K1's. Acceptance must include a graded tap after a K2 reply, not only text equality. Without this, K2 is the 'chatbot next to a board' the spec says it rejects, and the one LLM-load-bearing moment is a chat bubble.
- Replace the later_features novelty ranking: 'Sit in their chair' is Dr. Wolf's flip-sides feature and must not be presented as 'the skeptic's strongest novelty claim'. Name the real bet, which the brief's own 'not in the world' sentence already points at ('a memory of what you personally keep getting wrong'): the question that remembers, i.e. K1 choosing which look to point at from this player's hold log and phrasing it as 'This one again' (currently iteration three), promoted to iteration two with the hold log it already has. That is the only item on the list no incumbent covers.
- Make the silence testable, because silence is the real contrast with Play Coach ('commentary and tips every move') and Dr. Wolf (praise and commentary). The spec's thesis says the coach says nothing on ninety percent of moves, yet 'Looked.' fires every time the opponent attacks something and he saves it, and 'Free knight. You looked.' on every free capture; in a beginner game that can be a third of his moves. Add a quiet budget to success_criteria and a scenario: over a scripted 30-move game with no holds, the coach line and chip change at most N times (pick N, e.g. 4), and the chip never fires twice in a row. Otherwise the differentiator evaporates in play and the product becomes Dr. Wolf with better typography.
