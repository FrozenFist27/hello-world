# Hold On

*Chess that holds your move and asks. You answer on the board.*

This is the MVP specification the team converged on (seven partners ideated, cross-reviewed, a product lead synthesized, four adversarial critics attacked, the lead revised once). It is the build contract: builders implement what is here, and the acceptance criteria are what the headless tests check. The raw record is in `product/IDEATION.md` and `product/ideation.json`.

## Thesis

Under 1000 a game is decided by a piece left where it can be taken, and the moment that matters is the half-second before it lands. Several products already act in that half-second, and Hold On must be honest about them: Dr. Wolf (Chess.com) interrupts a hanging move, asks "are you certain?", offers a take-back and will flip the board, but it names the problem and the answer is yes or no; Shredder's and Fritz's coaches warn and show the better move; Fritz Assisted Analysis, the ChessUp board and the Chess.com Highlighter colour squares safe or unsafe before the drop, which is showing, not asking; Chess.com's Play Coach withholds the move and lights pieces, but it talks on every move and nothing is ever held; Chessiro answers a typed question with Stockfish-grounded arrows, after the game; Maia, Noctie and Shredder's weak levels make human-shaped mistakes. What none of them does, as far as our search found, is the thing this product is: a real game in which a move that would give material away does not land, one question is asked whose only answer is a square you tap, the coach never names the move or the answer, and on every other move it says nothing. The pedagogy is not invented either; it is lesson 0001 in this repo ("Before you let go of the piece: the same three looks, from their chair", Steps Method Step 1 and Fischer), verified at depth 20, turned into a thumb gesture. The engine and chess.js decide everything that is chess, instantly and before Claude is asked; Claude does only what a template cannot: read the player's own stated plan ("I'm attacking his king"), say what is true in it and what breaks it, and choose which of the position's real looks to ask him next, which the page then asks and grades. The opponent is designed to make the look decisive, and measured: against a 1320-rated Stockfish that hangs pieces the recipe scores 8 of 10, and against the same engine prevented from ever leaving a piece hanging it scores 0.5 of 10 and resigns in seven games (scratchpad bot_sim4.py and bot_sim5.py, ten games each): you lose this opponent by giving pieces away and you beat it by not doing so, which is exactly the habit the hold builds. It is buildable in a session because the whole beat works with zero Claude calls, every number comes from the vendored Stockfish WASM already proven in headless Chromium (verdict searches 1-42 ms), and every test position is verified at depth 20 in this repo.

## The new interaction

The held move that asks, and that you can argue with on the board. You drop a piece. Most of the time it lands and the opponent replies inside a second with nothing said. When the engine and chess.js agree that the move hands over material to a one-move reply (a piece left where a pawn or a cheaper piece takes it, a piece of his already under attack and not answered, a free piece of theirs walked past, a mate in one allowed or missed, a stalemate thrown away), the piece does not land: it stays lifted over the destination with a soft shadow, the square breathes amber, every other square dims, and the serif line asks one question whose answer is a square: "Hold on. If the knight lands there, what takes it? Tap it." Nothing is marked, so the look is his. He taps. chess.js already holds the answer set, so the tap is graded at once: "Yes, the pawn. A knight for a pawn." or "The knight on c6 could too. Is there something cheaper? Tap it." or "Not that one. Look at the pawns next to it." Only after he has answered do the two buttons appear: "Take it back" slides the piece home and records nothing against him; "Play it anyway" commits, the opponent takes every time, and the sentence that follows tells the truth about what he saw. "Show me" plays the capture as a ghost on his own board, a large "-2" floats off his edge, a beat, and everything slides back. And "Hear me out", the only text field in the product and only inside a hold: he types "im attacking his king", the line echoes his words while it looks, and the coach answers his plan in two sentences ("You are, and the bishop on c4 already points at the pawn in front of their king. But it is their move first, and the pawn on d6 takes the knight.") then the page asks the look Claude chose from the position's real looks, in its own graded words: "Tap a square where the knight is safe." and chess.js grades that tap too. Three things make this neither a chatbot beside a board nor Fritz on a web page: the coach speaks only inside a hold the engine decided, it never names the move or the answer square, and every question it asks, including the one after his argument, is answered by a tap that the page grades. Every chess fact in every sentence was computed before Claude was asked and is checked again on the way out; a wrong square cannot reach the board.

## Who it is for

Sambudh first: played before, rating once under 1000 and forgotten, studied nothing, opens 1.e4, loses to pieces taken for free and one-move threats, phone in one hand, ten to fifteen minutes, and closes any app that shows an evaluation bar or a red "??". Then everyone like him: adult returners and late beginners under about 1000 who want to play rather than read, know how the pieces move, and would never open a lesson. Not titled players, not puzzle grinders, not children who cannot read a sentence. He is the first tester, so the first screen must show the product working within fifteen seconds with no manual, no dialog and no engine wait.

## Core loop

1. 1. 'Your move.' While he thinks, the Worker runs the pre-search on his position (ucinewgame; go depth 14 multipv 2; Skill Level 20) so evalBefore, the best move, any free capture available to him and any mate in one for him are known before he touches a piece. While the engine is still cold (first seconds on a phone) his moves commit with no verdict and no reward; the player's own move never waits.
2. 2. He moves by tap-tap (tap a piece, tap a square; drag is not in v1; promotion auto-queens). On the drop the piece slides 150 ms into a pending state (input locked, nothing drawn, a faint dot on the piece only after 250 ms). chess.js builds the post-move fact sheet synchronously; the engine is stopped (its late bestmove discarded by search id; if the stopped pre-search is shallower than depth 8 a go depth 10 runs first) and runs go depth 12 multipv 2 on the new position: 1-42 ms at depth 10 in the probe, under 150 ms at depth 12, under 600 ms on a mid-range phone.
3. 3. Clean (no category below matches, or cpLoss under 200): the move commits with the last-move tint, and the opponent replies after at least 400 ms and inside a second, nothing said. Two rewards, under a quiet budget: if his capture took a piece worth two or more that chess.js (attacked and undefended, or attacked by a cheaper piece) and the pre-search (best move was that capture) both called free: a green ring for 900 ms and 'Free knight. You looked.'; if before his move a piece of his worth two or more was hanging per chess.js and after it nothing of his hangs: 'You looked. The pawn was on your bishop.' in the line for two seconds. At most four reward lines per game, never on consecutive moves; everything else is silence.
4. 4. Hold: gate.js, a pure function, takes the first matching category in this order and fires only when a one-move look explains the loss (a positional loss of any size never holds): allowed_mate (the reply line is mate in 1 against him); missed_mate (the pre-search had mate in 1 for him and his move is not mate); ignored_attack (cpLoss >= 200, the reply captures a piece of his worth two or more that is NOT the piece he moved, chess.js says it is attacked and undefended or attacked by a cheaper piece; sub-case 'already': it was so before his move; sub-case 'lost its guard': it became so because of his move); hanging_after_move (cpLoss >= 200, the first move of either reply line captures the piece he just moved, chess.js confirms the mechanism, net loss two or more); free_piece_ignored (the pre-search's best move captures a piece chess.js calls free, worth two or more, cpLoss >= 200, and he played something else); allowed_stalemate (chess.js says his move stalemates while he is five or more points ahead). The piece stays lifted, the square breathes amber, the board dims, the action bar stays EMPTY, and the template question for the category appears at 0 ms: 'Hold on. If the knight lands there, what takes it? Tap it.' / 'Hold on. Before you take there: what takes back? Tap it.' / 'Hold on. One of yours is already under attack. Tap it.' / 'Hold on. Something of yours just lost its guard. Tap it.' / 'Hold on. Something of theirs is free right now. Tap it.' / 'Hold on. Their queen has a check next move. Where does it land? Tap it.' / 'Hold on. You have a checkmate on the board. Tap the square.' / 'Hold on. After this, can their king move at all? Tap their king.' The link row shows 'Show me' and, when Claude is available, 'Hear me out'.
5. 5. He answers by tapping a square, graded by chess.js against the category's answer set (never Claude's): hanging: the enemy squares that can take the piece, cheapest best; ignored_attack: his attacked piece's square, with the attacker's square as the partial answer ('That is the pawn. Now tap the piece it reaches.'); free_piece_ignored: the free piece; allowed_mate: where their mating piece lands; missed_mate: where his mating piece lands; allowed_stalemate: their king. Right: 'Yes, the pawn. A knight for a pawn.' and a green ring. Partial: 'The knight on c6 could too. Is there something cheaper? Tap it.' Wrong: one hint ('Not that one. Look at the pawns next to it.'); a second wrong tap glows the answer square and names it ('The pawn on d6.'). Only now, or after 8 s with no tap, does the action bar fill: [Take it back] (ink, focused) [Play it anyway] (quiet). The first hold this browser ever shows adds one sentence after the tap: 'Take it back costs nothing; play it anyway is real.'
6. 6. 'Show me': the engine's refutation (the capture, and the recapture when the exchange has one) plays as translucent ghosts on a second piece layer while the real pieces dim; a 28 px serif '-N' (net piece points) rises off his edge of the board; a beat; everything slides back. No words, no Claude. Any tap aborts and rewinds it. The first float a browser sees is named once in words.
7. 7. 'Hear me out' (the one text field, only inside a hold, only when sample is available): a single-line input opens in the action bar, placeholder 'Say what you were going for', with the note 'The first time, your browser will ask whether the coach may think. That is all it is.' On send the input closes, his own words echo in the line in muted ink under a soft 'Looking...' pulse, and one quick-tier call returns {say, ask, squares}. The validated 'say' (two sentences, at most 20 words: what is true in his plan, then the one fact that breaks it) appears with up to three squares marked, followed by the page's own question for the look Claude chose from the ones chess.js offered for this hold ('Tap a square where the knight is safe.'), and his next tap is graded against that look's answer set. One reply per hold; the link then disappears. This is the first Claude call of every view, so the consent dialog lands on an explicit tap over a stable held state.
8. 8. He decides. 'Take it back': the piece slides home, nothing is recorded against him, the cached pre-search is reused, 'Your move.' 'Play it anyway': the move commits, the opponent plays its true best reply (depth 12, Skill Level 20) so the punishment is certain, and one sentence tells the truth about his look: 'There it goes. A knight for a pawn. You saw it coming; that counts.' after a right or partial tap, 'There it goes. A knight for a pawn. Next time, the pawns first.' after two wrong taps. That piece-and-square pair is not held again this game, and after the second play-anyway the gate is quiet for the rest of the game EXCEPT for allowed_mate, missed_mate, allowed_stalemate and any hold whose net loss is five points or more. Both buttons abort any pending Claude call; the hold is logged {fen, san, category, taps, outcome, coachShown}.
9. 9. The opponent (Stockfish 18 lite WASM in the Worker; options confirmed present: Skill Level, UCI_LimitStrength, UCI_Elo, MultiPV, Hash): first a full-strength look (Skill Level 20, go depth 8 multipv 2): if the top move is a capture that leads the second line by 150 cp or more, or a mate, it plays it, so every piece he leaves is taken, every time. Else, from its third move, with probability GIFT_RATE = 0.12, the gift: chess.js enumerates its legal moves and keeps those after which one of its own minor pieces is attacked and undefended or attacked by a pawn, that give no check, hang nothing worth five or more and allow no mate in one; up to six are checked at depth 10 (the player's best reply must be that capture, gaining 200 cp or more and staying under +900); the first that passes is played. Else the sampled move: Skill Level 3, go depth 2 (Lichess level-one shape), re-picked up to twice if chess.js finds a rook or queen of its own hanging or a mate in one for the player afterwards, then the full-strength top move. Book for Black's first replies to 1.e4 (eight lines, data/book.json, each verified under 50 cp loss at depth 18) so the opening never waits on warm-up. Measured over ten games each (seed 11): 2.5 verified gifts and 3.4 natural hangs per game, 97 plies per game, scores 8/10 against Stockfish at UCI_Elo 1320 and 0.5/10 (seven resignations) against the same engine prevented from leaving a piece hanging.
10. 10. Ends. The bot resigns when its own evaluation is -900 or worse for three consecutive bot moves after its sixth move, unless the player has a queen or a rook and the bot has only king and pawns, in which case it plays on so he can deliver the mate: 'Black gives up. You are a rook and a bishop ahead and it knows it.' (material in piece words from chess.js). When he is -900 or worse for three consecutive moves the line offers once: 'This one is gone. Start again?' [Start again] [Keep playing]. A 'Give up' link sits in the header at all times, confirmed in the action bar ('Give up this one? [Yes] [Keep playing]'). Mate: 'Checkmate. Well played.' / 'That one got away.' Draws: 'A draw.'
11. 11. Close card under the board, in units he can feel, numerals on the card and words in the voice: 'I stopped your hand 3 times; you took it back twice. Pieces given away: 1 (last game 4). Free pieces you took: 2.' Each hold as one caption line in piece words ('Knight to e5, a pawn was waiting: taken back.'), one button [Again]. Counts and holds go to localStorage.
12. 12. The next game opens on the start position with one remembered line templated from the stored categories ('Two holds last game, both a pawn you did not see. Watch the pawns.'), zero Claude calls, and GIFT_RATE steps down one notch (0.12 to 0.08) only after a game with zero pieces given away.

## MVP features (each with its acceptance test)

### The board

A static 64-cell CSS grid of squares under an absolutely positioned piece layer where each piece is a focusable <button class=pc data-square> placed by transform: translate() with a 150 ms transition (0 under prefers-reduced-motion), so moves, take-backs and the future flip animate for free. Twelve flat SVG silhouettes as inline <symbol>s used by <use>, built to one rule: a 100x100 viewBox, a shared 64-unit base, 6-unit strokes, one line per silhouette (pawn a ball on a flared stem; rook three merlons on a straight tower; knight a single-curve head facing left with one mane notch; bishop a mitre with a short diagonal cut; queen five points on a wide crown; king a cross on a domed crown), two hours capped; if that slips, Lichess's cburnett set (CC BY-SA 3.0) is vendored with attribution in app/LICENSE.md. No Unicode glyphs (tofu in headless Chromium). Input is tap-to-select then tap-to-move only in v1 (pointerup within 8 px and 300 ms is a tap; touch-action: none on the board; drag is iteration two). Legal squares as faint dots, captures as rings, last move as a translucent tint, coordinates as 11 px Plex Sans letters inside the a-file and first-rank squares. Promotion auto-queens in v1. The board is min(100vw - 32px, 62vh) with 16 px gutters at 400 px; keyboard focus is visible on every piece and button.

**Why in the MVP.** The board is the whole interface; every answer, from the engine or Claude, lands on its squares. The hold's lift, the ghost layer and the take-back slide all come from the same transform-driven piece layer, and the piece set is the only element with no reference in the repo, so its construction rule and time cap are written down.

**Acceptance.** In scripts/app_check.js at 1280 px and 400 px, light and dark: four screenshots with no horizontal overflow, zero console errors (an inline favicon <link rel=icon href='data:,'> prevents the 404 the probe logged), zero page errors, zero failed requests. document.querySelectorAll('.pc').length === 32 on the start position; every .pc is a button with a visible focus ring. A scenario taps .pc[data-square=e2] then the e4 cell and asserts window.__app.fen() begins with 'rnbqkbnr/pppp1ppp/8/8/4P3/8/PPPP1PPP' and that the e2 piece's transform changed. With page.emulateMedia({reducedMotion: 'reduce'}) the computed transition-duration on .pc is '0s'. A 46 px crop of each piece at 400 px is included in the screenshot set for a legibility check.

### The opponent

engine.js: Stockfish 18 lite in one Worker behind a serialized UCI queue with search ids (a new go waits for the previous bestmove after stop; a late bestmove from a stopped id is discarded). bot.js per bot move: (1) setoption Skill Level 20; go depth 8 multipv 2; if the top move is a capture leading the second line by >= 150 cp, or a mate, play it (punish-always); (2) from the bot's third move, with probability GIFT_RATE (0.12; 0.08 after a clean game), enumerate legal moves with chess.js, keep those after which a bot minor piece is attacked and undefended or attacked by a pawn, giving no check, hanging nothing worth >= 5, allowing no mate in one (chess.js enumerates the player's replies); verify up to six at depth 10 (the player's best reply is that capture, gains >= 200 cp from his side, and his eval stays under +900); play the first that passes; (3) else setoption Skill Level 3; go depth 2; if chess.js finds a bot rook or queen hanging or a mate in one for the player afterwards, re-pick (twice), then the full-strength top move; (4) the move slides no sooner than 400 ms after his. Play-it-anyway replies: Skill Level 20, go depth 12. Book: data/book.json, eight Black lines against 1.e4 (…e5; 2.Nf3 Nc6; 3.Bc4 Nf6; 3.Bb5 a6; 2.Qh5 Nc6 3.Bc4 g6; 2.Bc4 Nf6; and from the tutorial position 4.d3 Be7, 4.Nc3 Bc5, 4.O-O Bc5, 4.d4 exd4, 4.Ng5 d5), each verified at depth 18. Resignation: bot-side eval <= -900 for three consecutive bot moves after move six, unless the player has a queen or rook and the bot has only king and pawns. The self-play script (scratchpad bot_sim5.py, python-chess plus the native Stockfish) is ported to scripts/bot_sim.py and ships with the repo; GIFT_RATE, SKILL and SAMPLE_DEPTH are three named constants.

**Why in the MVP.** The reviewers' self-play proved the old recipe (80 cp sampler, gifts from inside the six best lines) was an engine with noise: 6-0 against a 1320 engine with zero gifts in six games. The new recipe is measured, not described: the gift enumeration makes 'Free knight. You looked.' reachable (2.5 verified gifts per game), punish-always makes 'Play it anyway' a lesson rather than a gamble, and the held-1320 experiment (0.5/10, seven bot resignations) shows that a player who looks beats it.

**Acceptance.** Headless: 6...d6 position (r1bq1rk1/ppp1bppp/2np1n2/4p3/2B1P3/2NP1N2/PPP2PPP/R1BQ1RK1 w - - 0 7), play('Nxe5'), tap('d6'), action('anyway'): Black's reply is dxe5 10 times out of 10; Ruy after 3...a6, O-O, tap('b5'), anyway: axb5 10/10. A scenario plays 1.e4 and asserts the book reply arrives within 2 s with the line empty. With window.__app.state().giftDue forced true on a quiet middlegame position, the bot's move leaves a minor piece attacked-and-undefended or attacked by a pawn per chess.js and state().lastGift.verified === true, or no gift is recorded. A position with Black a queen down, played through three bot moves via play(quiet moves), ends with #board.dataset.state === 'over' and the line containing 'gives up'. Offline: python3 scripts/bot_sim.py 10 (about 25 s) reports gifts >= 2.0 per game, mean plies <= 100, zero sampled moves that hang a rook or queen or allow mate in one, a score between 5 and 9 of 10 against UCI_Elo 1320 at depth 8, and at most 2 of 10 against the held 1320.

### The silent gate

gate.js exports verdict(pre, post, factsBefore, factsAfter, gameState) as a pure function with no DOM and no engine. Evaluations are converted to the player's side (Stockfish reports side-to-move; mate N maps to +/-(10000-N)); cpLoss = evalBefore - evalAfter. Category table in priority order with the exact test and answer set: allowed_mate (post line 1 is mate 1 against him; answer: the reply's destination); missed_mate (pre line 1 is mate 1 for him and his move is not mate; answer: the mating move's destination); ignored_attack (cpLoss >= 200; the first move of post line 1 or 2 captures a piece of his worth >= 2 that is not the moved piece; after his move chess.js says it is attacked and undefended or attacked by a cheaper piece; 'already' if that was true before his move, else 'lost_guard'; answer: that piece's square; partial: the attacker's square); hanging_after_move (cpLoss >= 200; the first move of post line 1 or 2 captures the moved piece; chess.js confirms attacked and undefended or attacked by a cheaper piece; net loss >= 2 counting the recapture; answer: the squares of every piece that can take it, cheapest marked best; question variant 'what takes back' when his move was a capture); free_piece_ignored (the pre-search's best move is a capture whose target chess.js calls free and worth >= 2; cpLoss >= 200; answer: that square); allowed_stalemate (chess.js isStalemate after his move and he is >= 5 points up; answer: their king). Suppressed for a piece-and-square pair already played anyway this game, and after the second play-anyway, except allowed_mate, missed_mate, allowed_stalemate and any hold with net loss >= 5. ucinewgame precedes every pre-search so fixed-depth evaluations do not drift between runs (all test positions have >= 70 cp of margin over the threshold).

**Why in the MVP.** The gate is the coach's band belief encoded as a rule: under 1000 only material lost to a one-move look matters, and a hold that fires on positional losses is a nag by game three. The table resolves the contradiction every critic found: the old priority made ignored_attack unreachable; now the discriminator is which piece the reply captures, and the chess master's first look (what did their last move attack) comes before the look from their chair.

**Acceptance.** All via window.__app.load(fen) then play(san), asserting #board.dataset.state and dataset.hold. 6...d6 position: Nxe5 -> held, hanging_after_move; Bxf7+ -> held, hanging_after_move (capture variant, answer {f8 best, g8}); h3 -> committed, Black replies, window.__sampleCalls.length === 0. Tutorial position 3...Nf6 (engine warm, tutorial flag set): Bxf7+ held, Nxe5 held, d3 committed. Ruy after 3...a6 (r1bqkbnr/1ppp1ppp/p1n5/1B2p3/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQK2R w KQkq - 0 4): O-O held with hold=ignored_attack and state().hold.answerSquares deep-equal ['b5'] with partial ['a6']; Ba4 committed. Placement after 7...O-O (r2q1rk1/ppp2ppp/2np1n2/2b1p3/2B1P1b1/2PP1N1P/PP3PP1/RNBQ1RK1 w - - 1 8): Nbd2 held free_piece_ignored (the reply Bh5 is not a capture; the category does not need one); hxg4 committed with 'You looked'. ### The hold

When the gate fires, the board root gets data-state=held and data-hold=<category>; CSS lifts the piece (translateY(-6px) scale(1.06), 0 8px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.28) shadow, full opacity), breathes the destination amber (1.6 s inset glow; static ring under reduced motion), dims every other square with --dim (a black overlay in both themes so the dark board gets darker, not lighter), and swaps input to answer mode. The template question appears at 0 ms in the serif line, built from data/copy.json keyed by category x the pieces involved x whether a pawn is among the takers; 'Hold on.' is always the first two words and no template names a move or an answer square (copy_check.js runs the validator's SAN and square rules over the whole table). The action bar is EMPTY until he has tapped (right, partial, or two wrongs) or 8 s have passed (300 ms under window.__app.fast); then [Take it back] (ink, --on-coach text, focused) and [Play it anyway] (quiet) appear. The link row under the line (24 px, 44 px tap targets) holds 'Show me' and, when sample is available and the view is not rate-limited, 'Hear me out'. The line box is a fixed four lines (19 px / 1.35 = 103 px on phone, 21 px = 113 px on desktop) in every state, so the board never moves.

## Claude: the LLM design

**Grounding rule.** Two gates, one before and one after, and no tools in the beat. Before: facts.js computes the fact sheet from chess.js and the Worker (the held move, what takes it and what takes back with piece names, the net loss, the category, the answer squares, the looks available for this hold with their answer sets kept page-side, the squares that may be named, the pieces on them) and the prompt contains nothing else; Claude never evaluates a move, never decides whether to hold, never types a FEN, and never asks a question the page cannot grade, because the question after a K2 reply is the page's own words for a look chess.js computed. After: validate.js is an ordered pipeline: (1) every [a-h][1-8] token must be in the allowed set (K1: the fact-sheet squares, and never an answer square inside if_wrong; K2: the fact-sheet squares, every occupied square and the held piece's legal destinations), and a piece word before 'on <square>' must match the piece chess.js has there; those tokens are then masked; (2) on the masked text a SAN-shaped token (a piece letter, an 'x', an '=' or O-O) outside the listed moves rejects; (3) any remaining digit rejects; (4) blunder, mistake, inaccuracy, '??' (assembled from char codes) reject; (5) word and sentence caps, a question mark in K2's say, an ask outside the offered set, or more than three squares reject. The spec's two example outputs pass this pipeline (checked by a unit test), which the old regex failed. A rejected reply is replaced by the template silently and window.__coachRejects increments. Grading of every tap uses chess.js's own answer set; Claude's text is only ever words on the line and marks on squares that exist in the fact sheet.

**Without Claude.** Identical game minus one link. When claude.use('sample') settles null (any browser outside the viewer, local testing, a friend's copy), when the viewer declines, or on any error: the gate fires exactly the same, the lifted piece and the amber square appear, the template question for the category shows, the tap is graded by chess.js with template follow-ups built from piece names, Show me plays the refutation, both buttons work, the close card shows the counts, and the next game opens with the templated remembered line. ## The engine

Stockfish 18 lite (app/vendor/stockfish-18-lite-single.js + .wasm, 7.3 MB, single-threaded; its uci output lists Threads, Hash, MultiPV, Skill Level 0-20, UCI_LimitStrength, UCI_Elo 1320-3190, confirmed this session) in one Web Worker created at load and warmed with uci, isready and ucinewgame, behind engine.js: a serialized command queue with search ids, an info-line parser (depth, multipv, score cp or mate, pv), search(fen, {depth, multipv, skill}) resolving with per-line scores, pvs and the bestmove, and stop() marking the current id stale so its late bestmove is discarded; a new go is sent only after the previous bestmove has arrived (the engine ignores a go mid-search). chess.js 1.4.0 does everything instantaneous: legality, SAN and UCI, FEN, game over, moves({verbose: true}) for dots and rings, attackers() per square (it reports defenders too, verified in the vendored source) for the fact sheet, isStalemate, and the mate-in-one and hanging-piece enumerations the bot's guards use. All evaluations are converted to the player's side (mate N maps to +/-(10000-N)). The schedule per move pair: (1) IDLE, player to move: setoption Skill Level 20; ucinewgame; go depth 14 multipv 2 on the current FEN as the pre-search (depth 12 took 11-243 ms in the probe), yielding evalBefore, the best move, whether it is a free capture or a mate in one. (2) On drop: stop (a stop 50 ms into the pre-search still returned depth 10 in 92 ms; if the stopped depth is under 8 a go depth 10 runs first), chess.js applies the move on a scratch board and builds the post-move facts synchronously, then go depth 12 multipv 2 on the post-move FEN (depth 10 took 1-42 ms; budget under 150 ms headless, under 600 ms on a mid-range phone); evalAfter is negated to the player's side, the reply and whether it is a capture, check or mate 1 come from line 1, and hanging_after_move also accepts the capture as the first move of line 2. (3) gate.js verdict. (4) Clean: commit; the opponent's move as in bot.js (full-strength depth 8 multipv 2 look, then the gift enumeration with up to six depth-10 checks, then Skill Level 3 go depth 2 with chess.js guards), all well under 300 ms headless and shown no sooner than 400 ms after his move so it reads as a move; then back to (1). Held: no search runs while the hold is open; Take it back reuses the cached pre-search; Play it anyway runs Skill Level 20 go depth 12 for the true best reply. Cold engine: while the Worker has not answered readyok, the player's moves commit with no verdict and no reward, and the bot's reply comes from the book or waits with a 6 px dot and 'Warming up the other side...' at the opponent's edge; the tutorial never touches the engine. Resignation: bot-side eval at or below -900 for three consecutive bot moves after move six, except when the player has a queen or rook and the bot has only king and pawns. Show me: the ghost line is the reply pv's first one or two plies, played from a scratch Chess; the '-N' is chess.js's material difference across those plies. Determinism: ucinewgame before every pre-search, Hash left at 16 MB; the probe's run-to-run drift was 15-25 cp and every test position has at least 70 cp of margin. The whole thirteen-pair acceptance batch ran in 1.9 s in headless Chromium with a 368 ms warm-up. Every number the gate uses, every refutation Show me plays, every answer square the tap is graded against, every gift the bot leaves and every fact in Claude's prompt comes from this pipeline; the engine never speaks to the player and Claude never evaluates a position.

## Information architecture

- **Tutorial (first visit only)** — The board on the verified Italian after 3...Nf6, White at the bottom; the serif line 'Try one. Most people take the pawn in front of the king here. Go on.'; an empty link row; an empty action bar; a 44 px header with 'Hold On' small at left and, at right, the 'Give up' link and the theme dot (a 12 px circle, filled --ink in light, hollow in dark, 44 px target; unset follows the system until the first tap). Primary action: Make a move (tap a piece, tap a square).
- **Your move (idle)** — The board with the last move tinted; the line 'Your move.' (or, at the start of games two onward, the remembered line); an empty action bar; occasionally, within the quiet budget, the green ring with 'Free knight. You looked.' or 'You looked. The pawn was on your bishop.' for two seconds. Primary action: Make a move.
- **Pending (0-300 ms after a drop)** — The piece on its new square at full opacity, input locked, nothing drawn; a faint dot on the piece only if the verdict takes longer than 250 ms (a code path, not a tested state). Primary action: None; the page is deciding.
- **Hold, asking** — The piece lifted with a shadow over the destination, the destination breathing amber, every other square dimmed; the line 'Hold on. ...' with one question whose answer is a square; the link row with 'Show me' and, when available, 'Hear me out'; the action bar EMPTY. Primary action: Tap the square you think answers the question.
- **Hold, answered** — The same board with the tapped square ringed green (right or partial) or the hint line (wrong; after a second wrong tap the answer square glows and is named); the action bar now holds [Take it back] (ink, focused) and [Play it anyway]; the first hold a browser sees adds 'Take it back costs nothing; play it anyway is real.' Primary action: Take it back (primary) or Play it anyway.
- **Show me (inside a hold)** — Real pieces at 35 percent, ghost pieces playing the capture at 50 percent, the 28 px serif '-N' rising from his board edge (named once in words the first time), a beat, then everything slides back to the hold. Primary action: Watch; any tap rewinds.
- **Hear me out (inside a hold)** — A one-line input in the action bar with placeholder 'Say what you were going for', a send button and the note about the browser's one-time question; the board scaled to stay visible above the keyboard. On send: his words echo in muted ink under 'Looking...'; if this is the view's first call the platform's consent dialog sits over this stable state (sub-state 'asking permission'); then the coach's two sentences with up to three squares corner-marked, followed by the page's question for the chosen look; the input gone; the next tap graded. Primary action: Type one line and send; then tap the square the page asks for.
- **Opponent's move** — The board with the player's move committed; the line unchanged; the reply slides in between 400 ms and 1 s; a 6 px --muted dot at the opponent's board edge after 800 ms, and 'Warming up the other side...' as a 12 px caption there only when a bot move is truly waiting on engine warm-up. Primary action: Wait less than a second.
- **Play it anyway, punished** — The opponent's capture slides in; one honest sentence in the line ('There it goes. A knight for a pawn. You saw it coming; that counts.' or '... Next time, the pawns first.'); back to Your move. Primary action: Make a move.
- **Mercy** — After three moves at -900 or worse, once: the line 'This one is gone. Start again?' with [Start again] [Keep playing] in the action bar; or, from the header's 'Give up' link at any time, 'Give up this one? [Yes] [Keep playing]'. Primary action: Start again (or keep playing).
- **Game over and close card** — The final position; the line 'Checkmate. Well played.' / 'That one got away.' / 'Black gives up. You are a rook and a bishop ahead and it knows it.' / 'You gave up.' / 'A draw.'; the close card under the board: 'I stopped your hand 3 times; you took it back twice. Pieces given away: 1 (last game 4). Free pieces you took: 2.' with one caption line per hold in piece words; one button [Again]. Primary action: Again.
- **Coach unavailable (sample null, declined, or an error)** — Nothing visibly different: the same holds with template questions and follow-ups, the same Show me and buttons; the 'Hear me out' link is absent; after rate_limited the line says 'Coach is resting.' for a minute. Primary action: Make a move.

## Persistence

store.js exposes {get, set, append, list} and in the MVP is backed by localStorage only, every read and write wrapped in try/catch so a private window or blocked storage still plays: holdon.v1.tutorialSeen ('1'), holdon.v1.firstHoldSeen ('1'), holdon.v1.firstFloatSeen ('1'), holdon.v1.theme ('light' | 'dark' | unset, set by the header dot and written to data-theme on :root), holdon.v1.giftRate (the current notch), holdon.v1.games (the last five game summaries: {id, date, result, givenAway, holds: [{fen, san, category, taps, outcome, coachShown}], freeTaken, playedAnyway, plies}), holdon.v1.holds (the last thirty hold moments across games, the material for the remembered line and, in iteration two, for the question that remembers, 'Play it again from here' and 'Send this one'). Consent is per view and is never stored. Nothing about the current game is persisted mid-game (a reload starts a new game). Iteration two moves games and holds to the db + user capabilities at data/users/{self}/games and data/users/{self}/holds behind the same store.js interface, with localStorage kept as a mirror, because localStorage is per browser and Sambudh will switch from laptop to phone; the builder reads the artifact-capabilities skill for db's exact API before writing that path, noting that viewers of a public link who are not the owner never write.

## Risks

- Over-holding turns the coach into a nag: the thresholds (200 cp AND a capture or mate refutation AND a chess.js-nameable mechanism AND net two points) are enforced by the scenario tests, never by taste; the quiet-after-two rule with its mate and five-point exceptions and the never-the-same-square rule ship on day one.
- The opponent is now measured against an engine, not against a person: Skill Level 3 at depth 2 may still play moves that look alien, the gift enumeration may rarely qualify in closed positions, and against a player who hangs more than three pieces a game the punish-always rule wins almost every game early (a 1320 engine scored 2 of 10). The design's answer is the hold itself, the mercy offer and the give-up link; Sambudh's first three games and scripts/bot_sim.py decide SKILL, SAMPLE_DEPTH and GIFT_RATE, which are three named constants.
- Engine warm-up on a slow phone is 2-4 s plus a 7.3 MB download: the tutorial and the book cover it, but the first real verdict on a cellular connection may be skipped by the cold-engine policy, and the verdict budget must be measured on a real Android, not only in headless Chromium.
- Consent lands on the first Hear me out of every view; a viewer who declines never sees Claude for the rest of that view, and a viewer who never presses the link never sees Claude at all. That is accepted: the product plays identically, and the first week's coachShown counts say whether the words are missed.
- The quick tier may ignore the rules (name an unlisted square, add a number, ask its own question); the validator and the template make this invisible, but a high rejection rate would mean Claude never speaks; measure window.__coachRejects on real calls and tighten the prompt rather than loosen the validator.
- K1 is the weakest moment on the table: after consent it costs a call per hold for follow-up phrasing the template already does adequately; if the log shows it is rarely shown before his tap or rarely better, it is cut in iteration two and the call budget goes to the question that remembers.
- Typing on a phone: the keyboard covers the board unless the board scales with visualViewport (unverifiable in headless Chromium); the fixed four-line box and the 20-plus-8-word cap are measured only with a stand-in serif, so the first phone screenshot with Source Serif 4 loaded must confirm no clipping.
- The twelve silhouettes are two hours capped; if they are not legible at 46 px in the screenshots, the cburnett set ships with attribution and its CC BY-SA terms are noted beside the GPL engine in app/LICENSE.md.
- localStorage is per browser: the close card's 'last game' and the remembered line vanish when he moves from laptop to phone; db + user must follow in iteration two before the memory story is promised in copy.
- Scope creep toward Lichess: an eval bar, a move list, a strength slider and a rating are each one argument away; the copy test and the information architecture are the defence. Licensing: shipping Stockfish (GPLv3) means the page's own source should be published under a GPL-compatible licence; flagged in app/LICENSE.md, not solved.
